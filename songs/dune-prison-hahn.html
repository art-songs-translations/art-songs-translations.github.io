D'une prison

From a Prison · Reynaldo Hahn, Mélodie
Poem: Paul Verlaine (1844–1896), from Sagesse

Le ciel est, par-dessus le toit,
Si bleu, si calme!
Un arbre, par-dessus le toit,
Berce sa palme.
The sky, above the roof,
is so blue, so calm!
A tree, above the roof,
rocks its leafy crown.
La cloche, dans le ciel qu'on voit,
Doucement tinte.
Un oiseau sur l'arbre qu'on voit
Chante sa plainte.
The bell, in the sky that one sees,
gently rings.
A bird on the tree that one sees
sings its lament.
Mon Dieu, mon Dieu, la vie est là,
Simple et tranquille!
Cette paisible rumeur-là
Vient de la ville.
My God, my God, life is there,
simple and quiet!
That peaceful murmur there
comes from the town.
Qu'as-tu fait, ô toi que voilà
Pleurant sans cesse,
Dis, qu'as-tu fait, toi que voilà,
De ta jeunesse?
What have you done, O you there,
weeping without end,
say, what have you done, you there,
with your youth?
About the song. Verlaine's poem from Sagesse, the same poem that Fauré set as 'Prison': through a window the speaker sees sky, tree and bell tower, hears the town, and turns on himself with the last question. Hahn's title is 'D'une prison...' (from a prison); above the music the score prints a few lines of Verlaine's prose about a treetop he saw swaying above the wall, from his window, one August. Hahn sets the poem complete and brings back its first two lines at the end (not repeated here). 'Palme' is a palm-like spray of leaves; 'rumeur' is a distant murmur, not a rumour.
