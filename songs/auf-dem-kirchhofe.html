Auf dem Kirchhofe

In the Churchyard · Johannes Brahms, Op. 105, No. 4
Poem: Detlev von Liliencron (1844–1909)

Der Tag ging regenschwer und sturmbewegt,
Ich war an manch' vergess'nem Grab gewesen,
Verwittert Stein und Kreuz, die Kränze alt,
Die Namen überwachsen, kaum zu lesen.
The day went by, heavy with rain and storm-tossed;
I had been at many a forgotten grave:
weathered stone and cross, the wreaths old,
the names overgrown, scarcely legible.
Der Tag ging sturmbewegt und regenschwer,
Auf allen Gräbern fror das Wort: Gewesen.
Wie sturmestot die Särge schlummerten,
Auf allen Gräbern taute still: Genesen.
The day went by, storm-tossed and heavy with rain;
on all the graves the word froze: 'Has been.'
As the coffins slumbered, dead to the storm,
on all the graves there thawed, quietly: 'Healed.'
About the song. On a day of rain and storm the poet walks among forgotten graves. On all of them the word 'Gewesen' (has been, is no more) seems frozen; but as the dead sleep on, 'Genesen' (healed, recovered) quietly thaws there. The two words rhyme in German. 'Sturmestot' (literally 'storm-dead') is an unusual compound, read here as 'dead to the storm', beyond its reach; the line can also be read as an exclamation: 'How storm-dead the coffins slumbered!' Liliencron's own title, in his collected works, is 'Auf dem Kirchhof'.
