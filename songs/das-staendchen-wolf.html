Das Ständchen

The Serenade · Hugo Wolf, Eichendorff-Lieder, No. 4
Poem: Joseph von Eichendorff (1788–1857)

Auf die Dächer zwischen blassen
Wolken schaut der Mond herfür,
Ein Student dort auf der Gassen
Singt vor seiner Liebsten Tür.
Onto the rooftops, between pale
clouds, the moon looks forth;
a student there in the lane
sings at his sweetheart's door.
Und die Brunnen rauschen wieder
Durch die stille Einsamkeit,
Und der Wald vom Berge nieder,
Wie in alter, schöner Zeit.
And the fountains murmur again
through the quiet solitude,
and the forest down from the mountain,
as in the good old days.
So in meinen jungen Tagen
Hab' ich manche Sommernacht
Auch die Laute hier geschlagen
Und manch lust'ges Lied erdacht.
So in my young days
I too, on many a summer night,
played the lute here
and made up many a merry song.
Aber von der stillen Schwelle
Trugen sie mein Lieb zur Ruh,
Und du, fröhlicher Geselle,
Singe, sing' nur immer zu!
But from the quiet threshold
they carried my beloved to her rest;
and you, merry fellow,
sing, just keep on singing!
About the song. An old man hears a student serenading his sweetheart by moonlight and remembers his own serenades, before his beloved was carried from her quiet threshold to her rest. Wolf's score has 'Wolken schaut der Mond herfür' (the moon looks out), as in Eichendorff's Gedichte published by Duncker und Humblot; the editions of 1841 and 1843 read 'scheint' (shines). 'Lieb' is the beloved; 'die Laute schlagen' is to play the lute. Wolf repeats the last words ('sing' nur zu, immer zu!'), which we print once.
