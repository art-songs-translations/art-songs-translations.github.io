Regenlied

Rain Song · Johannes Brahms, Op. 59, No. 3
Poem: Klaus Groth (1819–1899)

Walle, Regen, walle nieder,
Wecke mir die Träume wieder,
Die ich in der Kindheit träumte,
Wenn das Naß im Sande schäumte!
Stream down, rain, stream down,
wake again for me the dreams
that I dreamed in childhood
when the water foamed in the sand!
Wenn die matte Sommerschwüle
Lässig stritt mit frischer Kühle,
Und die blanken Blätter tauten,
Und die Saaten dunkler blauten.
When the languid summer sultriness
lazily strove with fresh coolness,
and the shining leaves dripped with dew,
and the young crops turned a darker blue.
Welche Wonne, in dem Fließen
Dann zu stehn mit nackten Füßen,
An dem Grase hinzustreifen
Und den Schaum mit Händen greifen,
What bliss, in the running water
to stand then with bare feet,
to brush along the grass
and to catch the foam in one's hands,
Oder mit den heißen Wangen
Kalte Tropfen aufzufangen,
Und den neuerwachten Düften
Seine Kinderbrust zu lüften!
or with hot cheeks
to catch cold drops,
and to the newly awakened scents
to open one's child's breast!
Wie die Kelche, die da troffen,
Stand die Seele atmend offen,
Wie die Blumen, düftetrunken,
In dem Himmelstau versunken.
Like the flower cups that dripped there,
the soul stood open, breathing,
like the flowers, drunk with fragrance,
sunk in the dew of heaven.
Schauernd kühlte jeder Tropfen
Tief bis an des Herzens Klopfen,
Und der Schöpfung heilig Weben
Drang bis ins verborgne Leben.
With a shiver each drop cooled
deep down to the beating of the heart,
and the holy weaving of creation
pressed right into the hidden life.
Walle, Regen, walle nieder,
Wecke meine alten Lieder,
Die wir in der Türe sangen,
Wenn die Tropfen draußen klangen!
Stream down, rain, stream down,
wake my old songs
that we sang in the doorway
when the drops rang outside!
Möchte ihnen wieder lauschen,
Ihrem süßen, feuchten Rauschen,
Meine Seele sanft betauen
Mit dem frommen Kindergrauen.
I would like to listen to them again,
to their sweet, moist murmur,
and gently bedew my soul
with a child's devout awe.
About the song. The rain brings back the speaker's childhood: standing barefoot in the running water, catching the drops, singing in the doorway while it rained outside. Groth printed the poem in Hundert Blätter (1854), his collection of High German poems. Brahms sets 'In dem Himmelstau' (in the dew of heaven) where Groth wrote 'In den' (into the dew); the Peters edition prints Groth's reading. 'Wallen' is to flow or stream; 'das Naß' is poetic for water; the leaves 'tauten', dripped with dew (not 'thawed'); the young crops 'blauten', turning a darker, bluish green; 'Weben' (weaving) is also the quiet stirring of life.
