Quand je fus pris au pavillon

When I Was Caught in the Pavilion · Reynaldo Hahn, Rondels, No. 8
Poem: Charles d'Orléans (1394–1465)

Quand je fus pris au pavillon
De ma dame très gente et belle,
Je me brûlay à la chandelle
Ainsi que fait le papillon.
When I was caught in the pavilion
of my lady, so gracious and fair,
I burned myself at the candle
just as the moth does.
Je rougis comme vermillon
À la clarté d'une étincelle,
Quand je fus pris au pavillon
De ma dame très gente et belle.
I turned as red as vermilion
in the light of a spark,
when I was caught in the pavilion
of my lady, so gracious and fair.
Si j'eusse été esmerillon
Ou que j'eusse eu aussi bonne aile,
Je me fusse gardé de celle
Qui me bailla de l'aiguillon
Quand je fus pris au pavillon!
Had I been a merlin,
or had I had as good a wing,
I would have kept clear of her
who gave me the sting
when I was caught in the pavilion!
About the song. A rondeau by Charles d'Orléans: caught in his lady's pavilion, the poet burned himself at the candle like a moth. 'Pavillon' (a tent or canopy) rhymes with 'papillon' (moth or butterfly), and both words come from Latin 'papilio'. Hahn sets a modernised text; the 1842 edition of Charles d'Orléans's poems reads 'Quant je fus prins ou pavillon' and has 'Aussi flambant qu'une estincelle' (as fiery as a spark) where Hahn has 'À la clarté d'une étincelle' (by the light of a spark). An 'esmerillon' is a merlin, a small falcon; 'bailler' is old French for 'to give', and an 'aiguillon' is a goad as well as a sting; 'gente' means noble, gracious.
