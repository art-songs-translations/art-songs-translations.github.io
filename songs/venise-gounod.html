Venise

Venice · Charles Gounod, Mélodie
Poem: Alfred de Musset (1810–1857)

Dans Venise la rouge,
Pas un bateau qui bouge,
Pas un pêcheur dans l'eau,
Pas un fallot.
In red Venice
not a boat is stirring,
not a fisherman on the water,
not a lantern.
La lune qui s'efface
Couvre son front qui passe
D'un nuage étoilé,
Demi-voilé.
The moon, fading away,
covers her passing brow
with a starry cloud,
half-veiled.
Tout se tait, fors les gardes
Aux longues hallebardes,
Qui veillent aux créneaux
Des arsenaux.
All is silent except the guards
with their long halberds
who keep watch on the battlements
of the arsenals.
Ah! maintenant plus d'une
Attend, au clair de lune,
Quelque jeune muguet,
L'oreille au guet.
Ah! Now more than one woman
is waiting, in the moonlight,
for some young dandy,
her ear on the alert.
Sous la brise amoureuse
La Vanina rêveuse
Dans son berceau flottant
Passe en chantant;
Under the loving breeze
dreamy Vanina,
in her floating cradle,
glides past singing;
Tandis que pour la fête
Narcissa qui s'apprête
Met devant son miroir
Le masque noir.
while, for the festivities,
Narcissa, getting ready,
puts on before her mirror
the black mask.
Laissons la vieille horloge,
Au palais du vieux doge,
Lui compter de ses nuits
Les longs ennuis.
Let us leave the old clock
in the old Doge's palace
to count out for him, of his nights,
the long tedium.
Sur sa mer nonchalante,
Venise l'indolente
Ne compte ni ses jours
Ni ses amours.
On her nonchalant sea
indolent Venice
counts neither her days
nor her loves.
Car Venise est si belle
Qu'une chaîne sur elle
Semble un collier jeté
Sur la beauté.
For Venice is so beautiful
that a chain upon her
seems a necklace thrown
upon beauty.
About the song. Night in Venice: no boat stirs, the guards keep watch, women wait for their lovers and the city counts neither its days nor its loves. Gounod sets five of the seventeen stanzas of Musset's 'Venise' (dated 1828 in his collected works: the 1st, 5th, 9th, 10th and 15th) and four stanzas that Musset wrote for the song, which are not in the poem as printed: a gentler version of the stanza about Vanina ('Sous la brise amoureuse'), a reworking of the 11th ('Tandis que pour la fête / Narcissa qui s'apprête' for 'Pour le bal qu'on prépare, / Plus d'une qui se pare'), 'Sur sa mer nonchalante' and 'Car Venise est si belle'. The score spells 'fallot' (lantern), Musset 'falot'. A 'muguet' (literally lily of the valley) was a young dandy; 'fors' means 'except'.
