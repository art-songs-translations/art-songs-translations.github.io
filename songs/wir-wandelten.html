Wir wandelten

We Walked · Johannes Brahms, Op. 96, No. 2 (1884, published 1886)
Poem: Georg Friedrich Daumer (1800–1875), after a Hungarian poem

Wir wandelten, wir zwei zusammen,
Ich war so still und du so stille;
Ich gäbe viel, um zu erfahren,
Was du gedacht in jenem Fall.
We walked, we two together;
I was so quiet, and you so quiet;
I would give a great deal to know
what you were thinking at that moment.
Was ich gedacht, unausgesprochen
Verbleibe das! Nur Eines sag' ich:
So schön war alles, was ich dachte,
So himmlisch heiter war es all'.
What I was thinking, unspoken
let it remain! Only one thing I will say:
everything I thought was so beautiful,
it was all so heavenly serene.
In meinem Haupte die Gedanken,
Sie läuteten wie gold'ne Glöckchen:
So wundersüß, so wunderlieblich
Ist in der Welt kein and'rer Hall.
In my head my thoughts,
they rang like little golden bells:
so wondrously sweet, so wondrously lovely
is no other sound in the world.
About the song. Two people walked together in silence; the speaker will not say what he was thinking, only that his thoughts rang like golden bells. Daumer printed the poem in the Hungarian ('Magyarisch') section of Polydora (1855), his 'world-poetry songbook' of German versions of poems from many lands. Brahms sings 'läuteten' (rang, of bells) where Daumer has 'lauteten' (sounded). 'In jenem Fall' means on that occasion; 'Hall' is a sound or peal.
