In Waldeseinsamkeit

In the Solitude of the Forest · Johannes Brahms, Op. 85, No. 6 (published 1882)
Poem: Carl von Lemcke (1831–1913)

Ich saß zu deinen Füßen
In Waldeseinsamkeit;
Windesatmen, Sehnen
Ging durch die Wipfel breit.
I sat at your feet
in the solitude of the forest;
a breathing of wind, a longing,
passed widely through the treetops.
In stummem Ringen senkt' ich
Das Haupt in deinen Schoß,
Und meine bebenden Hände
Um deine Knie ich schloß.
In silent struggle I lowered
my head into your lap,
and my trembling hands
I clasped around your knees.
Die Sonne ging hinunter,
Der Tag verglühte all,
Ferne, ferne, ferne
Sang eine Nachtigall.
The sun went down,
the glow of the day faded out entirely,
far, far, far away
a nightingale sang.
About the song. A lover remembers sitting at the beloved's feet in the forest until the sun went down and a nightingale sang far away. Lemcke printed the poem in his Lieder und Gedichte (1861); Brahms's publisher Simrock and the early editions title the song 'In Waldeseinsamkeit', and it is often listed as 'In Waldeinsamkeit'. 'Windesatmen' is the breathing of the wind; 'breit' (broad) may describe how it spread through the trees or, in folk-song word order, the broad treetops themselves. In 'Der Tag verglühte all', 'all' means entirely. Brahms repeats the last line.
