L'ombre des arbres

The Shadow of the Trees · Claude Debussy, Ariettes oubliées, No. 3 (1885–87)
Poem: Paul Verlaine (1844–1896), from Romances sans paroles

L'ombre des arbres dans la rivière embrumée
Meurt comme de la fumée,
Tandis qu'en l'air, parmi les ramures réelles,
Se plaignent les tourterelles.
The shadow of the trees in the misty river
dies away like smoke,
while up in the air, among the real branches,
the turtledoves lament.
Combien, ô voyageur, ce paysage blême
Te mira blême toi-même,
Et que tristes pleuraient dans les hautes feuillées
Tes espérances noyées!
How much, O traveller, this pallid landscape
mirrored you, pallid yourself,
and how sadly, in the high foliage,
your drowned hopes were weeping!
About the song. The last of Verlaine's 'Ariettes oubliées': a traveller sees his own pallor and his drowned hopes in a misty river landscape. The score keeps Verlaine's epigraph from Cyrano de Bergerac: the nightingale at the top of an oak sees itself in the river below, believes it has fallen in, and is afraid of drowning. 'Mira' (from 'mirer') means 'mirrored, reflected'. Ditson's 1904 edition, like the OpenScore transcription, repeats 'noyées' at the end; the Fromont edition (1913 printing) sings it once, as here.
