La mort des amants

The Death of Lovers · Claude Debussy, Cinq poèmes de Baudelaire, No. 5 (1887–89)
Poem: Charles Baudelaire (1821–1867), from Les fleurs du mal

Nous aurons des lits pleins d'odeurs légères,
Des divans profonds comme des tombeaux,
Et d'étranges fleurs sur des étagères,
Écloses pour nous sous des cieux plus beaux.
We shall have beds full of light fragrances,
divans as deep as tombs,
and strange flowers on shelves,
that bloomed for us under lovelier skies.
Usant à l'envi leurs chaleurs dernières,
Nos deux cœurs seront deux vastes flambeaux,
Qui réfléchiront leurs doubles lumières
Dans nos deux esprits, ces miroirs jumeaux.
Vying with each other to spend their last warmth,
our two hearts will be two vast torches
that will reflect their twofold light
in our two minds, those twin mirrors.
Un soir fait de rose et de bleu mystique,
Nous échangerons un éclair unique,
Comme un long sanglot, tout chargé d'adieu;
One evening made of rose and mystic blue
we shall exchange a single flash of light,
like a long sob, heavy with farewell;
Et plus tard un Ange, entr'ouvrant les portes,
Viendra ranimer, fidèle et joyeux,
Les miroirs ternis et les flammes mortes.
and later an Angel, half-opening the doors,
will come, faithful and joyful, to bring back to life
the tarnished mirrors and the dead flames.
About the song. A sonnet from 'La mort', the last section of Les fleurs du mal: two lovers imagine their death as a last exchange of light, and an angel who will one day rekindle it. Debussy follows the revised text printed in the 1868 edition ('Un soir fait de rose', 'Et plus tard un Ange'), where the first edition of 1857 has 'Un soir plein de rose' and 'Et bientôt un Ange'. The oldest prints of the song reached here (Durand and Ditson, 1904) have 'tout chargé d'adieu' (laden with farewell), printed here; Baudelaire's editions and Durand's later engraving have 'd'adieux' (farewells), which sounds the same. 'Un éclair unique' is a single flash, and perhaps also a matchless one; 'à l'envi': vying with each other.
