Minnelied

Love Song · Johannes Brahms, Op. 71, No. 5 (1877)
Poem: Ludwig Heinrich Christoph Hölty (1748–1776), as revised by Johann Heinrich Voss (1751–1826)

Holder klingt der Vogelsang,
Wenn die Engelreine,
Die mein Jünglingsherz bezwang,
Wandelt durch die Haine.
More lovely sounds the birdsong
when the angel-pure one,
who conquered my young heart,
walks through the groves.
Röter blühen Tal und Au,
Grüner wird der Wasen,
Wo die Finger meiner Frau
Maienblumen lasen.
Redder bloom valley and meadow,
greener grows the turf
where the fingers of my lady
gathered May flowers.
Ohne sie ist alles tot,
Welk sind Blüt' und Kräuter;
Und kein Frühlingsabendrot
Dünkt mir schön und heiter.
Without her everything is dead,
blossom and herbs are withered;
and no spring sunset
seems lovely and bright to me.
Traute, minnigliche Frau,
Wollest nimmer fliehen,
Daß mein Herz, gleich dieser Au,
Mög' in Wonne blühen!
Dear, lovely lady,
may you never flee,
so that my heart, like this meadow,
may bloom in bliss!
About the song. 'Minne' is the old word for courtly love, and the poem borrows that language: 'Frau' for the poet's lady, 'minniglich' (lovely, worthy of love). Brahms used the text of Johann Heinrich Voss's 1804 edition of Hölty, which already alters Hölty's poem of 1773, and changed Voss's 'Wann' to 'Wenn' and 'blühet' to 'blühen'. 'Wasen' is turf or sward (the Ditson edition of 1903 prints 'Rasen', lawn); 'lasen' means gathered.
