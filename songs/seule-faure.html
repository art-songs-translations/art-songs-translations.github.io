Seule!

Alone! · Gabriel Fauré, Op. 3, No. 1
Poem: Théophile Gautier (1811–1872)

Dans un baiser, l'onde au rivage
Dit ses douleurs;
Pour consoler la fleur sauvage,
L'aube a des pleurs;
Le vent du soir conte sa plainte
Aux vieux cyprès,
La tourterelle au térébinthe
Ses longs regrets.
In a kiss, the wave tells the shore
its sorrows;
to console the wild flower
the dawn has tears;
the evening wind tells its lament
to the old cypresses,
the turtledove to the terebinth
its long regrets.
Aux flots dormants, quand tout repose,
Hors la douleur,
La lune parle, et dit la cause
De sa pâleur.
Ton dôme blanc, Sainte-Sophie,
Parle au ciel bleu,
Et, tout rêveur, le ciel confie
Son rêve à Dieu.
To the sleeping waters, when all is at rest
except grief,
the moon speaks and tells the cause
of her pallor.
Your white dome, Hagia Sophia,
speaks to the blue sky,
and the sky, all dreaming, confides
its dream to God.
Arbre ou tombeau, colombe ou rose,
Onde ou rocher,
Tout, ici-bas, a quelque chose
Pour s'épancher...
Moi, je suis seule, et rien au monde
Ne me répond,
Rien que ta voix morne et profonde,
Sombre Hellespont!
Tree or tomb, dove or rose,
wave or rock,
everything here below has something
to pour out its heart to...
I am alone, and nothing in the world
answers me,
nothing but your voice, dismal and deep,
dark Hellespont!
About the song. A woman alone by the Hellespont (the Dardanelles) hears everything in nature confide in something, the wave in the shore, the wind in the cypresses, the sky in God, while only the sea answers her. Gautier's poem, dated 1845 in his collected poems, is headed 'Dans un baiser, l'onde'; the title 'Seule!' (alone) is the song's. Fauré sets it complete, with one change: 'Aux vieux cyprès' (to the old cypresses) for 'Au vieux cyprès'. The terebinth is a Mediterranean tree; 'Sainte-Sophie' is Hagia Sophia in Constantinople.
