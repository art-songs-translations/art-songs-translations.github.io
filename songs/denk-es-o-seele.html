Denk' es, o Seele!

Think of It, O Soul! · Hugo Wolf, Mörike-Lieder, No. 39 (1888)
Poem: Eduard Mörike (1804–1875)

Ein Tännlein grünet wo,
Wer weiß, im Walde,
Ein Rosenstrauch, wer sagt,
In welchem Garten?
Sie sind erlesen schon,
Denk' es, o Seele,
Auf deinem Grab zu wurzeln
Und zu wachsen.
A little fir tree is growing green somewhere,
who knows, in the forest;
a rose bush, who can say
in which garden?
They are already chosen,
think of it, O soul,
to take root on your grave
and to grow.
Zwei schwarze Rößlein weiden
Auf der Wiese,
Sie kehren heim zur Stadt
In muntern Sprüngen.
Sie werden schrittweis gehn
Mit deiner Leiche;
Vielleicht, vielleicht noch eh'
An ihren Hufen
Das Eisen los wird,
Das ich blitzen sehe!
Two little black horses are grazing
in the meadow;
they go home to the town
with lively leaps.
They will walk at a slow pace
with your body;
perhaps, perhaps even before
on their hooves
the iron comes loose
that I see flashing!
About the song. A meditation on death: the fir and the rose that will grow on the soul's grave are already chosen, and the frisky black horses in the meadow will one day draw the hearse at a walking pace, perhaps before the shoes now flashing on their hooves have worked loose. 'Erlesen' means chosen; 'Leiche' is the dead body. Wolf sets Mörike's words unchanged; the doubled 'vielleicht' is Mörike's own.
