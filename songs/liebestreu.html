Liebestreu

Love's Constancy · Johannes Brahms, Op. 3, No. 1
Poem: Robert Reinick (1805–1852)

„O versenk', o versenk' dein Leid, mein Kind,
In die See, in die tiefe See!“
Ein Stein wohl bleibt auf des Meeres Grund,
Mein Leid kommt stets in die Höh'.
“Oh, sink, oh, sink your sorrow, my child,
in the sea, in the deep sea!”
A stone may well stay on the bottom of the sea;
my sorrow always comes up again.
„Und die Lieb', die du im Herzen trägst,
Brich sie ab, brich sie ab, mein Kind!“
Ob die Blum' auch stirbt, wenn man sie bricht,
Treue Lieb' nicht so geschwind.
“And the love that you carry in your heart,
break it off, break it off, my child!”
Though the flower dies when it is picked,
true love does not die so quickly.
„Und die Treu', und die Treu', 's war nur ein Wort,
In den Wind damit hinaus!“
O Mutter, und splittert der Fels auch im Wind,
Meine Treue, die hält ihn aus.
“And your faithfulness, your faithfulness, it was only a word;
out into the wind with it!”
O mother, even if the rock splinters in the wind,
my faithfulness will withstand it.
About the song. A mother tells her daughter to sink her grief in the sea, break off her love and throw her promise to the wind; the girl answers that her grief always rises again and that her faithfulness will outlast even a splitting rock. It opens Op. 3, the earliest of Brahms's song sets with an opus number. Reinick printed the poem in his Lieder (1844) under the same title. 'Brechen' is both to break off and to pick (a flower), which the second stanza turns on. Brahms repeats 'die hält ihn aus' at the end.
