Therese · Johannes Brahms, Op. 86, No. 1 (published 1882)
Poem: Gottfried Keller (1819–1890)

Du milchjunger Knabe,
Wie schaust du mich an?
Was haben deine Augen
Für eine Frage getan!
You milk-young boy,
why do you look at me so?
What a question your eyes
have asked!
Alle Ratsherrn in der Stadt
Und alle Weisen der Welt
Bleiben stumm auf die Frage,
Die deine Augen gestellt!
All the councillors in the town
and all the wise men of the world
are struck dumb by the question
that your eyes have asked!
Eine Meermuschel liegt
Auf dem Schrank meiner Bas':
Da halte dein Ohr d'ran,
Dann hörst du etwas!
A sea-shell is lying
on my aunt's cupboard:
hold your ear to it,
then you will hear something!
About the song. A woman teases a boy who looks at her too earnestly: no councillor or sage can answer the question in his eyes, but a sea-shell on the cupboard will tell him something. 'Milchjung' means young as milk, callow. 'Bas'' (Base) can be an aunt or a female cousin; in Swiss German, Keller's own speech, it meant first of all an aunt. Brahms set the poem as Keller printed it in his Neuere Gedichte (1851); for his collected poems Keller later rewrote the last stanza, in which an empty snail shell lies in the grass instead.
