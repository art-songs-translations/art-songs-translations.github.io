Der Musikant

The Musician · Hugo Wolf, Eichendorff-Lieder, No. 2
Poem: Joseph von Eichendorff (1788–1857)

Wandern lieb' ich für mein Leben,
Lebe eben, wie ich kann,
Wollt' ich mir auch Mühe geben,
Paßt es mir doch gar nicht an.
I love wandering dearly,
I just live as best I can;
even if I wanted to make an effort,
it does not suit me at all.
Schöne alte Lieder weiß ich;
In der Kälte, ohne Schuh'
Draußen in die Saiten reiß' ich,
Weiß nicht, wo ich abends ruh'.
Fine old songs I know;
in the cold, without shoes,
out of doors I strike the strings,
not knowing where I will rest at night.
Manche Schöne macht wohl Augen,
Meinet, ich gefiel' ihr sehr,
Wenn ich nur was wollte taugen,
So ein armer Lump nicht wär'.
Many a pretty girl makes eyes at me
and says she would like me very much
if only I were willing to be good for something
and were not such a poor ragamuffin.
Mag dir Gott ein'n Mann bescheren,
Wohl mit Haus und Hof versehn!
Wenn wir zwei zusammen wären,
Möcht' mein Singen mir vergehn.
May God grant you a husband
well provided with house and farm!
If the two of us were together,
my singing might well die away.
About the song. A wandering musician would rather sing barefoot in the cold than settle down, and wishes the girl who makes eyes at him a well-to-do husband: married, his singing would fade away. 'In die Saiten reißen' is to strike the strings hard; 'Lump' is a ragamuffin, a good-for-nothing. Eichendorff's editions of 1841 and 1843 print it as the first of several poems headed 'Der wandernde Musikant'. Wolf sets Eichendorff's words unchanged.
